# Voice and tone

How Meddle sounds. Read this before writing any copy. For approved lines, use [`messaging.md`](messaging.md). For word choices, use [`concepts.md`](concepts.md).

**The voice in one sentence:** a senior creative partner who takes your business seriously, doesn't take themselves too seriously, and tells you the truth, including the price.

## The voice follows the magic trick

The copy is 90% discipline (clear claims, business outcomes, short sentences) and 10% interference (one reframe, one wink, one italic payoff). If a paragraph has three jokes, cut two. If a page has no memorable line at all, it isn't finished.

## Five principles

### 1. Meddle on purpose
Take a position. Name the default that's holding the client back and say what we'd do instead.
- **Why:** Challengers hire us to help them *not* look like the incumbent. Neutral copy signals a neutral studio.
- **Where:** Headlines, case-study "challenge" and "strategy" sections, LinkedIn.
- ✅ "The category sells management. Armor Bands sells reclamation."
- ✅ "MFO didn't need a *louder* brand. They needed one that matched the caliber of what they'd already built."
- ❌ "We help brands navigate an evolving landscape."

### 2. Say the business thing
Talk in outcomes a CEO or CMO would put in a board deck: deposits, pipeline, referrals, hiring, credibility in the room.
- **Why:** "Businesses don't buy 'branding.' They buy solutions to their problems." It's our core belief (see positioning.md).
- **Where:** Everywhere, and especially services, proposals, and case-study results.
- ✅ "After the rebrand, their first surgeon meeting ran 90 minutes against a 60-minute agenda."
- ❌ "We created a stunning, elevated identity that truly resonates."

### 3. Precise first, playful second
Specificity earns the joke. State the plain fact, then allow one wink.
- **Why:** Humor on top of vagueness reads as filler. Humor on top of a concrete claim reads as confidence.
- **Where:** Sales pages, headers, social. Case studies and proposals stay mostly straight.
- ✅ "Fast. Cheap. *And* Good." followed immediately by "Complete visual identity in just two weeks, for just $30K."
- ✅ "We embed with your team, challenge your assumptions (and probably a few sacred cows)…"
- ❌ A pun in every heading.

### 4. Show your cards
Be transparent about price, timeline, scope, and fit. Say who we're *not* for.
- **Why:** The traditional agency hides its price and its juniors. Transparency is a differentiator, so it belongs in the copy.
- **Where:** Contact page, Sprints, proposals, intro-call follow-ups.
- ✅ "Our engagements start at $30,000, with comprehensive projects typically landing between $90K–$250K."
- ✅ "We don't often take on projects needing quick fixes or incremental improvements."
- ❌ "Contact us for a custom quote tailored to your unique needs." <!-- check-copy: ignore -->

### 5. Senior, not stuffy
Confident, plainspoken, warm. Write like someone with a decade-plus in the room who no longer needs jargon to sound smart.
- **Why:** Clients work directly with the founder. The copy should sound like that person, not like a committee.
- **Where:** Everywhere.
- ✅ "Sometimes the best identity work is knowing what not to touch."
- ✅ Form labels: "What's your name?" / "Your company name too please."
- ❌ "Leveraging best-in-class methodologies to drive synergistic brand outcomes." <!-- check-copy: ignore -->

## Sentence craft

- **Short declaratives, fragments for rhythm.** "No guesswork. No haggling. Just impressive work, lightning fast, for one clear price." Use one long sentence after a run of short ones so the rhythm doesn't get monotonous.
- **Reframes through contrast.** "X sells A. Y sells B." / "Not A. B." / "Present when you want it, invisible when you don't." Use at most one per section.
- **The rule of three,** when it's natural: "Fast. Cheap. And Good." / "Two brand options. Detailed deliverables. One unbeatable price."
- **Give the object a job.** "Orange does one job, and it does it loudly." Concrete verbs over abstract nouns.
- **Specific over generic.** Name the thing: "DC think tanks, biotech research organizations," not "a range of clients." Call out clichés you avoided: "generic nonprofit clichés (smiling children, clasped hands, etc.)".
- **Questions sparingly.** Use them rhetorically and for effect ("All three? At the same time? In THIS economy?"), not as lazy openers ("Are you looking for…?").

## Person and pronouns

- **"We"** is the studio voice: site, case studies, proposals, the brand's social accounts.
- **"I"** is Matt, in founder-bylined pieces: personal LinkedIn, Fractional Leadership, intro emails, and anything he signs.
- **"You"** is the client. Address them directly. Avoid "the client" in public copy.
- Don't mix "we" and "I" in the same piece.

## Tone by context

The voice stays constant. Tone shifts with the room:

| Context | Tone | Interference budget | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage / hero | Bold, provocative | 1 italic payoff | "It's time you seized an *unfair advantage*." |
| Sales page (Sprints) | Punchy, witty, comparative | 1 move: a headline wink carried by its italic (an aside may echo it) | "Fast. Cheap. *And* Good." |
| Services / process | Confident, clear | 1 italic phrase per sentence, max | "Through a process rooted in *careful discovery and bold imagination*…" |
| Case study | Narrative, insight-led, generous to the client | 1 reframe per section | "We didn't tear down what was already there. We sharpened it." |
| Contact / pricing / fit | Direct, respectful, transparent | None needed | "Budgets vary by stakes and scope." |
| Proposal / SOW | Precise, calm, senior | Title only | Section headers follow the deck title pattern: "Scope *& Timeline*" |
| LinkedIn / social | POV-first, conversational | 1 line that makes someone stop scrolling | Lead with a claim, not "Excited to share…" | <!-- check-copy: ignore -->
| Email (1:1) | Human, brief, warm | None | Short paragraphs, one clear ask. |
| Error / empty states | Light, helpful | Optional small wink | Keep the fix obvious. |

## Italic emphasis in copy

Italic *is* the brand's interference in words, so it follows strict rules (for the visual rules, see `../visual/typography.md`):

- **Headlines:** italicize exactly one word or short phrase, and make it the *payoff*, not the setup. "Executive *Summary*," "Fast. Cheap. *And* Good."
- **Statements and paragraphs:** at most one italic *move* per sentence. A matched pair naming the two halves of one idea counts as one move: "By merging *business strategy* with *exceptional design*." Never two sentences in a row that both lean on italics for their meaning.
- **The aside:** a short line set *entirely* in italic is allowed in exactly one place, directly under a display headline, talking back to it: "*All three? At the same time? In THIS economy?*" It's part of the headline's move, not a second one. Keep it under about 10 words.
- **Sign-off:** *Fortune favors the daring.* as a sentence is always Baskerville italic in sentence case, even inside an uppercase mono footer (override the uppercase). It goes in the footer or the final CTA band on the web, and closes decks and emails. As **meta** (deck headers, poster corners) it's set in mono uppercase on two lines, "FORTUNE FAVORS / THE DARING". Those are the only two forms.
- In Markdown, write `*word*`. In HTML, write `<em>word</em>`. It renders in Libre Baskerville Italic automatically.

## Mechanics

- **Numbers and money:** `$30K`, `$90K–$250K` (capital K, en dash, no spaces in money ranges) everywhere except contracts, SOWs, invoices, and legal text, where you write `$30,000` in full.
- **Spelled-out vs. numerals:** spell out small numbers in sentences and phrase-style lists, even next to a price ("a complete visual identity in two weeks, for just $30K", "Two brand options"). Use numerals in tables, comparison columns, stats, and data ("2 weeks to brand", "3–9 months or more").
- **Prices in copy:** state the public price or floor (from `facts.md`) whenever the piece is about hiring Meddle: services, Sprints, contact, proposals, and sales emails. Leave prices out of bios, boilerplates, and thought-leadership posts.
- **Ranges:** en dash. "2–3 months", "3–12 months".
- **Em dash** for asides and turns, with no spaces: "—whatever your project demands."
- **Widows:** never leave one word alone on the last line of a headline or statement. On the web, bind the last two words with `&nbsp;`.
- **Case:** sentence case for headlines and buttons in body copy. UPPERCASE only happens in mono labels, and the CSS handles it, so type labels in normal case.
- **Product names are capitalized:** Brand Sprints, Web Design Sprint, Fractional Leadership, Project-Based.
- **Company name:** "Meddle" in copy. "MEDDLE" only as the wordmark, never typed out in caps in running text. The legal name is "Meddle, LLC" and appears only in legal and document meta. <!-- check-copy: ignore -->
- **Oxford comma:** yes.
- **Exclamation points:** almost never. Confidence doesn't need them.
- **Emoji:** not in brand copy. The one exception on the site is ⚡ as a mono-label marker for an add-on ("⚡ Add a Web Design Sprint"). Don't add new ones.

## Quick self-check before you ship copy

1. Could a traditional agency have written this? If yes, rewrite it.
2. Is there exactly one memorable move (reframe, wink, or italic payoff)?
3. Does it name a business outcome or a concrete detail?
4. Are the numbers from `facts.md`?
5. Run `node scripts/check-copy.mjs <file>` to catch banned words.
